30 AI Prompts for Client Reporting

Turn raw data into polished client reports in minutes — not hours.

How to use this pack: Copy any prompt into Claude, ChatGPT, or your preferred AI. Replace the bracketed placeholders with your actual data. Each prompt is designed to produce client-ready output — not a rough draft you need to rewrite.


These prompts are organized by report type. Use them end-to-end for a full report, or pick individual prompts when you need one section fast.

💡 Pro tip: Paste your raw data (spreadsheet export, GA4 screenshot description, or ad platform summary) directly into the prompt. The more specific your input, the cleaner the output. Use [BRACKETS] as your fill-in markers.

Section 1: Executive Summary Prompts

5 prompts
Prompt 01 — Monthly Executive Summary
Write a concise executive summary for a client report for [CLIENT NAME], a [INDUSTRY] business. This month's results: [KEY METRIC 1], [KEY METRIC 2], [KEY METRIC 3]. The main win was [WIN]. The main challenge was [CHALLENGE]. Keep it to 4–5 sentences. Tone: professional, direct, no jargon. Avoid starting with "This month" or "We are pleased."
Use when: Opening a monthly performance report.
Prompt 02 — Quarter-in-Review Summary
Write a 3-paragraph quarterly summary for [CLIENT NAME]. Q[NUMBER] highlights: [LIST 3–5 KEY RESULTS]. Compared to last quarter: [WHAT IMPROVED], [WHAT DECLINED]. Strategic context: [WHAT WAS THE QUARTER'S FOCUS]. Paragraph 1: results. Paragraph 2: what drove performance. Paragraph 3: what this sets up for Q[NEXT QUARTER]. Tone: confident, analytical, not sales-y.
Use when: Quarterly business reviews.
Prompt 03 — Metrics-to-Story Converter
I have a list of raw metrics from this month for client [CLIENT NAME]. Transform these into a clear narrative that explains what happened, why it matters, and what it means for the client's business. Raw metrics: [PASTE METRICS]. Output: 2–3 short paragraphs. No bullet points. No percentages without context. Make it readable by a non-technical business owner.
Use when: You have data but no story yet.
Prompt 04 — Good News Framing
Rewrite this performance summary to lead with the wins before addressing the challenges. Current draft: [PASTE DRAFT]. Keep all the facts accurate. Don't spin or obscure the challenges — just sequence them after establishing momentum. Tone: honest and confident, not defensive.
Use when: A report leads with a problem and needs reframing.
Prompt 05 — TL;DR One-Liner
Summarize this client's month in a single sentence that a CEO would say to their board. The sentence must include the primary outcome and one reason why. Context: [PASTE REPORT SUMMARY OR KEY METRICS]. No more than 25 words. Avoid the words "leverage," "synergy," "robust," or "impressive."
Use when: You need a subject line, slide header, or Slack summary.

Section 2: Channel Performance Prompts

6 prompts
Prompt 06 — Paid Ads Performance Narrative
Write a performance narrative for [CLIENT NAME]'s paid advertising this month. Data: Spend: $[AMOUNT]. Impressions: [NUMBER]. Clicks: [NUMBER]. CTR: [%]. Conversions: [NUMBER]. CPA: $[AMOUNT]. ROAS: [NUMBER]. Compare to last month: [WHAT CHANGED]. Explain what the numbers mean in plain English for a business owner who doesn't know advertising. 3–4 sentences max.
Use when: Reporting on Google/Meta/LinkedIn ads.
Prompt 07 — SEO Monthly Update
Write a client-facing SEO update for [CLIENT NAME]. This month: Organic sessions: [NUMBER] ([CHANGE %] vs last month). Top performing pages: [PAGE 1], [PAGE 2]. Keyword rankings movement: [KEY CHANGES]. New content published: [PIECES]. Technical issues addressed: [LIST]. Keep it in plain language — no SEO jargon unless explained. Format: short paragraph summary + 3 bullet points with context.
Use when: Monthly SEO reporting.
Prompt 08 — Social Media Performance Report
Write a social media performance section for [CLIENT NAME]'s report. Platforms: [LIST PLATFORMS]. Key metrics this month: [PASTE METRICS]. Compare to last month: [CHANGES]. Top performing content: [DESCRIBE 1–2 POSTS]. What this tells us about the audience: [YOUR OBSERVATION]. Format: 2 paragraphs — what happened, then what it means for content strategy going forward.
Use when: Social reporting across multiple platforms.
Prompt 09 — Email Marketing Results
Summarize this month's email marketing performance for [CLIENT NAME]. Emails sent: [NUMBER]. Open rate: [%] (industry avg: [%]). Click rate: [%] (industry avg: [%]). Unsubscribes: [NUMBER]. Revenue attributed: $[AMOUNT] (if available). Best performing subject line: [SUBJECT]. What worked: [YOUR NOTES]. Write 3 short paragraphs: performance vs benchmarks, what drove results, one recommendation for next month.
Use when: Reporting on Klaviyo, Mailchimp, or similar.
Prompt 10 — Multi-Channel Rollup
Write a multi-channel performance rollup for [CLIENT NAME] for [MONTH]. Channels: [LIST CHANNELS]. Each channel's standout metric: [CHANNEL 1]: [METRIC]. [CHANNEL 2]: [METRIC]. [CHANNEL 3]: [METRIC]. Overall theme across channels: [YOUR OBSERVATION]. Write one paragraph per channel (3 sentences max each), then a 2-sentence "what this all means" conclusion. No filler language.
Use when: Client has 3+ active marketing channels.
Prompt 11 — Underperformance Explanation
Write a professional explanation for why [CHANNEL/METRIC] underperformed this month for client [CLIENT NAME]. Actual result: [RESULT]. Expected result: [EXPECTED]. Likely causes: [CAUSE 1], [CAUSE 2]. What we're doing about it: [ACTION 1], [ACTION 2]. Tone: accountable without being defensive. Don't make excuses. Don't catastrophize. Make the client feel informed and confident the team has a handle on it.
Use when: You need to explain a miss without losing client confidence.

Section 3: Insights & Recommendations

6 prompts
Prompt 12 — Top 3 Insights Generator
Review this month's data for [CLIENT NAME] and generate 3 non-obvious insights. Data: [PASTE DATA OR SUMMARY]. An insight is not a metric restatement ("traffic went up 12%"). An insight explains WHY something happened or WHAT IT IMPLIES for the business. Format each insight as: [OBSERVATION] → [WHY IT MATTERS] → [WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT]. Keep each to 2 sentences.
Use when: You want to look like a strategist, not a data monkey.
Prompt 13 — Next Month Recommendations
Based on this month's results for [CLIENT NAME], write 3 concrete recommendations for next month. Results summary: [PASTE SUMMARY]. Each recommendation must be: specific (not "improve SEO"), actionable (something the team can do), and tied to a result from this month. Format: Recommendation → Rationale → Expected outcome. No more than 3 sentences per recommendation.
Use when: Closing out a monthly report with forward momentum.
Prompt 14 — Opportunity Spotter
Look at this data from [CLIENT NAME] and identify one overlooked opportunity. Data: [PASTE DATA]. An opportunity is something performing above average that isn't getting proportional attention or investment. Explain: what the opportunity is, what the evidence shows, what would happen if we doubled down, and what the first move would be. 3 short paragraphs.
Use when: You want to bring something proactive to the report.
Prompt 15 — A/B Test Results Summary
Write a clear summary of an A/B test for [CLIENT NAME]. Test: [WHAT WAS TESTED]. Variant A: [DESCRIPTION] — Result: [METRIC]. Variant B: [DESCRIPTION] — Result: [METRIC]. Winner: [VARIANT]. Statistical confidence: [%] (if known). What this tells us: [YOUR INTERPRETATION]. What we'll implement: [DECISION]. Format: 4 short paragraphs. Client doesn't need to understand statistics — explain in business terms.
Use when: Reporting on landing page, ad, or email tests.
Prompt 16 — Trend Analysis
Analyze this 3-month trend for [CLIENT NAME] and explain what it means. Month 1: [KEY METRICS]. Month 2: [KEY METRICS]. Month 3: [KEY METRICS]. What's the direction? Is it a trend or a spike? What's likely causing it? What should the client expect if this continues? Write 2–3 paragraphs. Use plain language. If the trend is concerning, say so plainly without alarming them unnecessarily.
Use when: Showing 90-day performance patterns.
Prompt 17 — Competitive Context Framing
Write a brief competitive context note for [CLIENT NAME]'s report. Their result: [RESULT]. Industry benchmark: [BENCHMARK]. Closest competitor performance (if known): [DATA]. Help the client understand whether their performance is strong, average, or lagging relative to what's normal in their space. 2 sentences max. Don't be falsely reassuring — be accurate.
Use when: Adding market context to raw numbers.

Section 4: Goal Tracking & OKR Reporting

5 prompts
Prompt 18 — Goal Progress Update
Write a goal progress update for [CLIENT NAME]. Goal: [GOAL]. Target: [TARGET]. Current progress: [CURRENT]. Timeline: [START DATE] to [END DATE]. At current pace, will they hit the goal? [YES/NO/MAYBE]. If yes: what's driving it. If no: what needs to change. Format: 3 sentences — where we are, trajectory, action required. Tone: direct, not alarm, not cheerleading.
Use when: Tracking quarterly or annual goals.
Prompt 19 — OKR Status Table
Convert these OKR updates into a clean status table with a written summary. OKRs: [PASTE OKR LIST WITH CURRENT STATUS]. For each key result, assign a RAG status (Red/Amber/Green) based on the data. After the table, write a 2-sentence summary: overall health of the OKRs, one thing that needs immediate attention. Format the table in plain text with | separators.
Use when: Client uses OKRs or quarterly targets.
Prompt 20 — Milestone Celebration Note
Write a short milestone acknowledgment for [CLIENT NAME]. Milestone achieved: [MILESTONE]. What it took to get there: [CONTEXT]. Why it matters for their business: [SIGNIFICANCE]. Keep it genuine — 3 sentences. Don't be sycophantic. Don't pad it. Just mark the moment clearly and connect it to what it means going forward.
Use when: A significant goal is reached and deserves recognition.
Prompt 21 — Off-Track Goal Plan
Write a recovery plan section for [CLIENT NAME]'s report. Goal: [GOAL]. Current status: behind target by [AMOUNT/PERCENTAGE]. Root cause: [CAUSE]. Recovery plan: [STEPS]. Revised expectation: [NEW TARGET OR TIMELINE]. Tone: accountable, solution-focused, not apologetic. The client needs to trust you have a plan — make that clear in the writing.
Use when: A goal is off track and needs a clear response.
Prompt 22 — Annual Review Setup
Write an annual performance narrative for [CLIENT NAME] using this data: [PASTE FULL YEAR SUMMARY]. Structure: Year in one sentence → 3 biggest wins → 2 biggest challenges and what we learned → What this sets up for next year. Length: 4 short paragraphs. Tone: reflective and forward-looking. This will be read by the client's leadership team — make it worth their 90 seconds.
Use when: Preparing a year-end executive summary.

Section 5: Report Polish & Communication

8 prompts
Prompt 23 — Report Introduction
Write an opening paragraph for [CLIENT NAME]'s [MONTH/QUARTER] report. This period's overall theme: [THEME]. Tone I want: [PROFESSIONAL / WARM / DIRECT]. Don't use "we're excited to share" or similar empty phrases. Start with the most important thing that happened. 3 sentences max. The client should know what to expect from the report by the time they finish the first paragraph.
Use when: Opening any structured performance report.
Prompt 24 — Report Closing & Next Steps
Write a closing section for [CLIENT NAME]'s report. Include: 3 priority actions for next month (specific, numbered), the date of the next check-in, and a one-sentence forward momentum statement. Keep the actions genuinely actionable — not "continue to monitor." Sign off with confidence, not deference. No "please don't hesitate to reach out" language.
Use when: Closing a monthly or quarterly report.
Prompt 25 — Plain English Translator
Rewrite this section of a client report in plain English. The client is a business owner who is not a marketer. Remove all jargon. Explain any technical terms the first time they appear. Keep all the facts intact. Current text: [PASTE TEXT]. Target: a 45-year-old founder who understands business but not digital marketing. If a sentence takes more than one read to understand, rewrite it.
Use when: Your report has too much marketing speak.
Prompt 26 — Email Subject Line for Report Delivery
Write 5 subject lines for an email delivering [CLIENT NAME]'s [MONTH] performance report. The main result this month: [RESULT]. Make 2 of them data-led, 2 narrative-led, and 1 that creates mild curiosity. No clickbait. Avoid "Your [Month] Report is Ready." Each subject line max 60 characters.
Use when: Emailing the report to a client.
Prompt 27 — Slack Message Summary
Summarize [CLIENT NAME]'s performance report into a Slack message. Key results: [PASTE SUMMARY]. Format: 3 bullets (top win, main challenge, one thing happening next) + a single sentence overall. Keep it under 100 words. This will be read on mobile — short sentences, no walls of text.
Use when: Sharing a report update in a client Slack channel.
Prompt 28 — Presentation Speaker Notes
Write speaker notes for a 10-minute client report presentation for [CLIENT NAME]. Slides outline: [PASTE SLIDE TITLES OR TOPICS]. For each slide, write 2–3 sentences the presenter should say — not read from the slide, but expand on it. Transition lines between slides. Keep the overall arc: context → results → insights → what's next. Conversational, not scripted.
Use when: Presenting a report live or via Loom.
Prompt 29 — Client Question Response
A client asked this question about their report: "[CLIENT QUESTION]." The data that's relevant: [PASTE DATA]. Write a response that: answers the question directly in the first sentence, explains the why behind the answer, and ends with what we're doing about it (if action is needed). Tone: expert, confident, not defensive. Max 5 sentences.
Use when: A client pushes back or asks a pointed question.
Prompt 30 — Report Quality Checker
Review this client report draft and identify: (1) any claims made without supporting data, (2) any sections that are vague or could be misread, (3) any overpromises in the recommendations, (4) any jargon a non-marketer wouldn't understand. Report draft: [PASTE FULL DRAFT]. For each issue found, quote the problematic text and suggest a fix. Be direct — this is going to a paying client.
Use when: Doing a final QA pass before sending.
